Robotic Arm Joint Health Monitoring System — Design Explanation

The immediate safety stop uses deterministic rules rather than machine learning. In this demonstration, a torque reading of 100 Nm or more, temperature of 90 °C or more, or vibration of 9 mm/s or more triggers EMERGENCY_STOP. Lower warning limits (70 Nm, 70 °C, and 5 mm/s) trigger monitoring. These are illustrative values chosen to make the synthetic examples easy to understand; they are not real industrial safety limits. The stop check runs first, so an incorrect or unavailable predictive model cannot prevent a stop when a danger threshold is crossed.

Predictive wear detection uses a small, dependency-free nearest-centroid classifier trained on the labeled synthetic historical CSV. The `wear_label` column is 0 for normal and 1 for wear risk. It compares standardized torque, temperature, and vibration values with the two class centroids. Technician notes are scanned separately for maintenance terms such as grinding, overheating, backlash, and lubrication, as well as sensor-fault or tampering terms. After the safety check, suspected sensor faults result in MONITOR; otherwise a wear prediction or maintenance concern results in SCHEDULE_MAINTENANCE; warning-range readings result in MONITOR; and only an arm with no detected concern is marked SAFE.

The spoof defense checks for explicit sensor-fault/tampering notes, high torque paired with unusually low temperature and vibration, and torque that is nearly unchanged across five recent samples. These checks may detect simple inconsistencies or a flatlined sensor, but they cannot catch every attack. An attacker could spoof several sensors consistently or provide believable values that pass the rules. A real system would need authenticated sensor communications, independent safety-rated monitoring, secure logging, and testing with real failure and attack data. This project is a synthetic educational prototype and must not be used to control actual industrial machinery.
